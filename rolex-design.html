01 — Final Artwork

Gold, gloss, and control.

The final poster presents the watch as a polished product illustration: a centred gold Day-Date composition, soft bokeh background, white dial, metallic bracelet, magnified date window, crown, logo placement, and a short product note at the bottom. The result feels simple at first, but the build depends on many small Illustrator decisions.

Focus

The watch is placed vertically in the centre so the bracelet, case, dial, and crown read as one premium product object.

Finish

The gold surfaces use warm gradients and narrow highlight bands to create the polished metal look without relying on a photo.

Detail

The dial includes markers, hands, date glass, day window, logo text, and tiny certification lines to make the illustration feel complete.

Stage

For a beginner-stage competition, the biggest challenge was not only drawing the watch, but proving the construction through a full process sheet.

02 — Working Board

The process was the proof.

The working process image shows the illustration moving from simple structure into a complete gold product render. It includes early body construction, dial development, bracelet forms, logo lockup, date window detail, full outline preview, colour palette, and gradient palette.

03 — Build Details

From outline to shine.

The board is broken down here so the viewer can understand what was actually designed: not one flattened poster, but a layered illustration system. Each part had to be shaped, shaded, aligned, and finished by hand inside Illustrator.

Detail 01

Base geometry.

The case, bezel, and bracelet begin as controlled shapes. The early build focuses on proportion before luxury effects are added.

Detail 02

Dial system.

The watch face is built through rings, markers, hands, logo text, day window, date window, and small certification details.

Detail 03

Final render.

The completed watch uses layered highlights and shadows to make the metal feel dimensional, polished, and product-ready.

Detail 04

Outline preview.

The outline view reveals the real construction behind the clean surface — hundreds of vector shapes working together.

Detail 05

Bracelet rhythm.

The bracelet depends on repeated link shapes and gradient direction, creating a metallic rhythm across the strap.

Detail 06

Gradient logic.

The gradient palette shows the main trick of the design: every shiny material is controlled with dark-to-light transitions.

Colour and gradient palette.

The palette stays warm and controlled: deep browns for shadow, creamy whites for dial and text contrast, and multiple gold tones for the bracelet and bezel. The separate gradient blocks explain how the metallic look was achieved rather than hiding the method.

04 — Lesson

A beginner win that became evidence.

This project matters because it came early. It was not made with a mature portfolio mindset; it was made while learning the tool. But the competition result proved that careful construction, clean presentation, and strong process documentation can stand out even when the designer is still at the beginner stage.
